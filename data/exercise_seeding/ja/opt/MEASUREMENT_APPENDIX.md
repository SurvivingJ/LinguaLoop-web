# Measurement appendix — how the quality numbers in this write-up were produced

**Judge design.** Every arm is scored by a tool-free Claude call that reads each finished document (core + variants A/B) and lists defects with a JSON path, a severity and a reason; the score is computed by code, never by the model: `10 − 3·fatal − 1·major − 0.3·minor`, floored at 0. Fatal = the item cannot be used (wrong or second-correct answer, a "wrong" sentence that is actually correct, a sentence in another sense); major = misleading but usable (wrong explanation, unnatural but grammatical Japanese); minor = style. The rubric and model are frozen per campaign so that every arm is judged alike (`scratch_offline/nt_judge.py`; output `judge.<source>.v<rubric>[.<model>-<effort>][_tag].json`). The frozen reference judge is Opus/high; the campaign's screening judge is Opus/medium with rubric 3 (same rubric, cheaper model setting).

**Repeatability and resolution.** Judging the same documents twice (Opus/high, 23 senses × 9 arms, 144 repeated senses) gives per-sense pass-to-pass r = 0.75–0.91 and a per-sense noise of σ ≈ 1.1 points. The fatal count is the stable signal (same count in 61–96 % of senses); minors are noise (r 0.1–0.6). On 100 senses with Opus/medium (2 passes × 2 arms) σ = 0.92, r = 0.84–0.86, and the pass-to-pass shift of an arm mean is 0.09. The limiting term is not the judge but the document-to-document variance between two pipelines on the same senses (SD ≈ 2.4–3.3 points per sense), which shrinks only with more senses: **on 23 senses no difference under ~1.3 points is credible; on 100 senses the minimum credible single-pass difference is 0.55 (two Sonnet-class arms) to ~0.7 (Haiku vs Sonnet), 2·SE**; a second judge pass improves this by <0.05. All arm comparisons in the write-up are therefore 100-sense, single-pass, paired by sense id, and quoted with ± 2·SE. Fatal shares carry ± 0.08–0.10 at n = 100.

**Proxy validation.** Against the frozen Opus/high judge's two-pass mean on 115 senses: Opus/medium r = 0.80 per sense, arm-mean Pearson 0.96, bias within ± 0.5, one rank flip (a tied pair), at $0.048 vs $0.079 per sense; Sonnet/high r = 0.58, +2 to +4 lenient, three rank flips (rejected); Haiku/high r = 0.60, lenient by 1.2–2.5 (rank order only). Screening was therefore done with Opus/medium; finalists were re-judged once with Opus/high (independent model setting, same rubric) paired with the baseline: for the first finalist the two judges gave paired differences of −0.66 ± 0.65 (Opus/high) and −0.71 ± 0.59 (proxy) on the same 96 senses — a gap of 0.05.

**Rubric 3 vs 4.** Rubric 2 (original) penalised things no pipeline can change: duplicate gold senses, corpus-mined sentences, the given definition, polite です/ます in beginner sentences, level_1 blocks with 4–8 options (allowed by spec), and counted one unnatural anchor several times. Rubric 3 (used for the whole gold100 campaign) puts the given definition and duplicate senses out of scope, counts a root cause once, and restricts "fits the blank" fatals to plain/polite readings. Rubric 4 (validated on 29 documents, unused for scoring) also injects each sense's mined sentences into the prompt, allows 4–8 level_1 options and drops the polite-form rule: it removed 100 % of those three noise classes (e.g. 21 → 0 register minors on 13 docs) at unchanged cost and fatal counts. Residual known judge errors under rubric 3: ~20 % of fatals rest on pedantic alternative readings (honorific passive, が/の alternation); naturalness majors on mined sentences (~0.07 points/sense); these are constant across arms and do not affect paired comparisons.

**Integrity (staleness).** Each judged sense stores a hash of the document it judged (`doc_sha`) and a timestamp; a resumed judge re-judges only senses whose document changed, and `nt_experiment.py --stale` audits every arm (hash mismatch, document newer than judgement, relaunch signals). One relaunched arm was found with ~⅓ of judgements describing superseded documents and was excluded; every arm quoted here is 100 % hash-checked with zero stale records.

**Production QA (proposal).** Judge a random 10 % of each production batch (≥ 100 senses) with one Opus/medium rubric-3/4 pass (≈ $0.0065 per produced sense). Primary metric: share of senses with ≥ 1 fatal (Sonnet baseline 0.14–0.19; finalists 0.19–0.21), ± 0.10 at n = 100; secondary: mean score (2·SE ≈ 0.5). Trigger when the fatal share exceeds the accepted baseline p₀ + 2√(p₀(1−p₀)/n) (p₀ = 0.30 → 0.39) or the score drops > 0.6: re-author the flagged senses with the Sonnet fallback and re-sample; two consecutive triggers stop the pipeline. Track the validator's first-pass rate per batch as a free leading indicator, and re-anchor the proxy against Opus/high on 100 senses (~$8) monthly.

*Tools: `nt_experiment.py --passes | --reliability | --proxy | --defects | --loss | --stale | --confirm`; all judge calls are logged with cost in `nt_usage.jsonl`. Total judging spend for the campaign's measurement track: ≈ $81 (incl. three Opus/high confirmations).*
